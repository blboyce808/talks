ONR FY27 MURI · Topic 16 · Recurrent self-healing
Metals can heal. How do we make it happen with intent?
A metal under load either deforms or fractures, and both are permanent. Then, inside an electron microscope, we watched a fatigue crack in platinum weld itself shut. Now, in gold, cracks heal nearly as often as they grow.
Platinum: Barr et al., Nature 620, 552 (2023). Gold: Baker et al., in review at Nature Communications.
Press on the platinum result: Scientific American, 1 Oct 2023; Daily Beast, 20 Jul 2023; Fast Company, Elissaveta M. Brandon, 2023
Our goal is not a self-healing part. It is the science that makes recurrent healing designable.
Platinum, in situ TEM. The crack faces touch, then the crack heals. Scale bars 50 nm.
Figure: Barr et al., Nature 620, 552 (2023), Fig. 2d to f
New results in peer review. Gold with 249 nm grains, in situ TEM over 1.5 million cycles. Each dot is the change in crack length over 2,000 cycles: of 679 steps, 197 grew and 148 healed by more than 5 nm. Baker et al., in review at Nature Communications, Fig. 3h
The gap: no laws yet for recurrent healing
Biological
Living tissue heals again and again. Bio-inspired capsules heal a site only once.
Source: Toohey et al., Nat. Mater. 6, 581 (2007)
Metallurgical
Cracks cold-weld shut at the nanoscale, but never yet by design.
Architectural
Lattices recover their shape, but not their damaged material.

Hypothesis · why it will succeed, and how it could fail
Recurrent healing is set by geometry, not chemistry
Three core scientific questions
  1. Healing or just recovery? Does the architecture restore damaged material, not only its shape?
  2. Surface chemistry. Can metal faces rebond through oxide, in air and then in seawater?
  3. Healing exhaustion. How many insults before healing runs out, and can we predict it?
Stretch goal (S1): get stronger after insult, as bone does under load. H numbers refer to the benchmark table in the concept paper.

Approach · four thrusts, three specimen scales, three tiers of outcome
One model system, tested from polymer pilots to printed metal
Three tiers of outcome
AttainableIdeal outcome
1
Sense, then repair on command
Metal structures that sense and communicate their damage state, and heal when given an external stimulus.
2
Heal on their own
Energy stored in the architecture drives the reset and the rebond, with no outside help.
3
Go beyond healing: self-enhance
Each repair leaves the structure more resistant to the next attack, as bone does under load (S1).
Tested at three scales: polymer two-photon microlattices (Year 1), metal microlattices (opportunistic), millimeter-scale printed metal joints
Every prediction has a pass/fail benchmark. Example, H1: at least 95% of undamaged stiffness and strength after 1,000 insults (proposed threshold, concept paper benchmark table).

Who cares · what changes for the Navy
Structures that take a hit at sea and heal on their own
The deliverable is the science, not a component: design rules that let a printed metal part heal a predicted number of times, and report how many remain.
Joints that heal, release and rejoin, for modular payloads, field repair and parts too large to print in one piece
Shock, slamming and fatigue absorbed and recovered from, without a shipyard
Parts that can sense and communicate their damage state and how many more times they can heal, so they are replaced when worn, not on a fixed schedule
